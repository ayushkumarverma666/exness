# Ayush wins with characters, not takes

Ayush Kumar Verma's own data answers the core question more clearly than any industry study: **3 of his 24 posts produced 88% of his 168,714 impressions in the last 12 months, and all three were narrative or humor posts built around a concrete Indian-engineer character** (a friend who is a lead engineer at a 500-crore company, a fake "5 years of LLM experience" job posting, a father told that BTech now takes five years). All 21 hot takes, stat posts, explainers and B2B/GTM posts landed between 364 and 1,504 impressions. That pattern fits what the wider evidence says about 2026. LinkedIn now ranks posts with LLM-augmented sequence models that match post text to each viewer's history and explicitly suppress engagement bait, pods and generic AI content. Academic work shows that high-arousal emotion, practical utility and featuring other people raise sharing odds, while the final size of a cascade is largely unpredictable before posting. Indian tech virality clusters around salary, layoffs, AI anxiety and family and work-culture humor. X's open-sourced ranker and Hacker News culture reward specificity, conversation and author replies, and punish bait that triggers "not interested". The recommendation is to commit to three lanes (real career/money stories, hiring and workplace satire, and Hinglish engineering-life humor that ends with a professional payoff), post about 4–5 times a week, drop B2B/GTM content, treat MiroFish/OASIS as a brainstorming toy rather than a forecaster, and log every post so that the next 30 days produce evidence rather than anecdotes. Expect most posts to land near his ~900-impression median no matter what. The goal is to raise how often a post breaks out and how many followers each breakout converts.

## LinkedIn's 2026 feed matches posts to viewers and penalizes bait

LinkedIn's feed changed architecture twice in two years. The 2024 LiRank paper described deep ranking models optimized for "contribution rate" (likes, comments, reposts) and reported +0.5% member sessions in the feed ([arXiv 2402.06859](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.06859v2)). The 2025 360Brew work described a ~150B-parameter LLM ranker (Brew-XL) distilled for serving across more than 30 tasks ([arXiv 2501.16450](https://export.arxiv.org/pdf/2501.16450); [AI Engineer talk](https://ai.engineer/talks/360brew-llm-based-personalized-ranking-and-recommendation-hamed-firooz-and-maziar-sanjabi-linked)). On **12 March 2026 LinkedIn announced a rebuilt feed using "Generative Recommender" sequence models augmented with LLMs** ([LinkedIn newsroom](https://news.linkedin.com/2026/ImprovingTheFeed)). Engineering coverage describes LLM dual-encoder retrieval that replaced several separate retrieval systems, and a transformer ranker that reads **more than 1,000 of a member's past interactions** and scores each candidate against that history, pulling roughly 2,000 out-of-network candidates per request ([ByteByteGo](https://blog.bytebytego.com/p/how-linkedin-feed-uses-llms-to-serve); [ZenML](https://www.zenml.io/llmops-database/next-generation-feed-ranking-with-llms-and-sequential-transformers)). Many creator blogs blame "360Brew" for reach drops, but the date of its feed rollout is contested and not officially confirmed ([pettauer.net](https://pettauer.net/en/linkedin-360brew-semantic-visibility-2026/)).

The practical consequence is that **reach beyond a creator's network now depends on whether the text semantically matches what a viewer already engages with**, not on network hacks. This explains Ayush's demographic shift: when his story and satire posts broke out, viewers were three times more likely than his followers to be senior engineers at 10,000+ employee companies. The model found an audience whose history matched "Indian software career" content. It found none for GTM posts aimed at founders, a group that makes up about 5% of his followers and viewers.

LinkedIn has been unusually explicit about what it punishes. The March 2026 announcement named "comment to agree" prompts, videos that don't match the post text, comment automation, engagement pods and unauthorized third-party tools ([SocialPilot recap](https://www.socialpilot.co/blog/linkedin-algorithm)). On pods, a LinkedIn spokesperson said the goal is "to make engagement pods entirely ineffective" and that flagged content has its reach limited ([Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-vows-to-take-action-against-engagement-pods-fake-engagement/804970/)). In May 2026 LinkedIn said suspected AI slop would not be deleted, but its reach would be depressed, **"often not allowing it to be seen beyond a poster's first-degree contacts"**, and generic AI comments would be targeted too ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2174163/linkedin-doesnt-want-your-ai-slop-anymore/); [Social Media Today](https://www.socialmediatoday.com/news/linkedin-wants-to-limit-the-reach-of-ai-generated-content/820935/)). For Ayush, who writes about AI, this is a direct risk: polished, templated "AI engineer" takes read as generic, and his specific, voice-heavy story posts do not.

Third-party data puts the reach environment in context. AuthoredUp's 621,833-post study found **average personal-profile reach fell 34% in 2025**. Text posts declined least (−34% median) and video declined most (−72%) ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm)). Van der Blom's ~1.8M-post 2025 report is cited for views down roughly 47–50% year over year, though recaps disagree on the engagement and follower figures ([contentin.io](https://contentin.io/blog/why-97-of-linkedin-creators-lost-half-their-reach-in-2025/)). Polls inflate reach (Metricool's 2026 study shows almost 3x reach per post) but AuthoredUp puts their engagement multiplier at only 0.37x ([Metricool 2026 PDF](https://metricool.com/wp-content/uploads/Linkedin-Study-2026-EN.pdf); [contentin.io stats](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-content-statistics/)). Documents and carousels show the highest engagement rates in Socialinsider data, about 7% ([meet-lea benchmarks](https://meet-lea.com/en/blog/linkedin-engagement-metrics-benchmarks)). Hashtags showed no reach effect in AuthoredUp's eight-month study ([AuthoredUp](https://authoredup.com/blog/linkedin-algorithm)). A peer-reviewed study of 991 posts found that **tagging people raised expected reactions by about 15%** and substantially increased comments ([Usera & Durham 2025](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/23294906251336710)). A van der Blom analysis is cited for a median reach cut of about 18.8% from a body link, but LinkedIn has never confirmed a link penalty ([contentin.io glossary](https://contentin.io/glossary/external-link-penalty/)).

Much popular LinkedIn lore has no traceable source: comments worth "15x" a like, a 60–90 minute "golden hour", "31–60 seconds of dwell for maximum distribution", and edit penalties ([meet-lea.com](https://meet-lea.com/en/blog/linkedin-algorithm-explained)). The only official support for early-engagement effects is a LinkedIn News editor's reported remark that ranking watches early engagement, relevance and network connections ([Megan Cornish Substack](https://megancornish.substack.com/p/a-week-as-a-man-a-conversation-with)). Timing evidence is weak too. Buffer's 4.8M-post study puts the best window at about 3–8 pm on weekdays, but that sample is global and US-heavy ([Buffer](https://buffer.com/resources/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/)), and India-specific advice (Tue–Thu 10 am–12 pm IST, or 8–10 am and 6–8 pm for personal brands) comes from vendor blogs without data ([WsCube Tech](https://www.wscubetech.com/blog/best-time-to-post-on-linkedin/)). For accounts under roughly 20k followers, the median engagement rate per impression sits around **2.2–2.7%** ([contentin.io](https://contentin.io/blog/linkedin-engagement-benchmarks/); [ConnectSafely citing AuthoredUp](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/linkedin-engagement-rate-calculator-guide-2026)).

## Research says emotion and people move shares, but virality defies forecasting

Three decades of virality research reduce to two lessons for a creator: some content features reliably shift the odds, and none of them makes outcomes predictable.

On features, Berger and Milkman's study of New York Times "most e-mailed" articles found that **high-arousal emotions (awe, anger, anxiety) increase sharing, low-arousal sadness reduces it, and practical utility and interest also raise it**. Their reported effects are roughly +30% odds for awe, +34% for anger, +21% for anxiety and +30% for practical utility per standard deviation ([Berger & Milkman PDF](https://faculty.wharton.upenn.edu/wp-content/uploads/2011/11/Virality.pdf)). Moral-emotional language spreads too, but the original estimate of +20% retweets per word shrank to **+12% (IRR 1.12) in a 2025 meta-analysis of 27 studies** by the original team ([Kellogg summary](https://www.kellogg.northwestern.edu/faculty/research/detail/2025/estimating-the-effect-size-of-moral-contagion-in-online)). Out-group language raised shares by about 67% per word in political Facebook and Twitter posts ([Rathje et al., PNAS](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC8256037/)). In 22,743 Upworthy headline experiments, **each negative word raised click-through by about 2.3%**, and sad words had the strongest effect ([Robertson et al., Nature Human Behaviour](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10202797/)). Most of that evidence comes from news and politics, though. On LinkedIn, outrage costs professional reputation. The emotions that fit Ayush's lane are anxiety (about placements, layoffs and AI), amusement at absurdity, awe at a surprising career outcome, and practical utility.

The only peer-reviewed LinkedIn content study adds a key detail. Across posts from 1,001 users, **interpersonal posts and posts highlighting others' achievements drew the most comments and reactions, while business and expertise posts were reposted more** ([Usera, Cox & Walker 2026, SAGE Open](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440261428891); [Phys.org](https://phys.org/news/2026-05-highlighting-linkedin-engagement.html)). His 110k post was literally a post about another person.

On prediction, the literature is sobering. Martin, Hofman, Sharma, Anderson and Watts showed that the best models explain well under half the variance in Twitter cascade size, that realistic upper bounds sit close to that, and that **before a post goes live, the author's past success dominates content features** ([arXiv 1602.01013](https://www.arxiv.org/abs/1602.01013)). Cheng et al. found that cascades become predictable only after early resharing is observed, with speed and early breadth as the key signals ([arXiv 1403.4608](https://pdf.arxiv.org/pdf/1403.4608)). Even in Upworthy's randomized tests, most headline variants show no statistically reliable winner ([arXiv 2609.25010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25010)). Strategy should therefore be judged on distributions across many posts, and creators should test big framing changes (character vs abstraction, a number vs none, Hinglish vs English) rather than wording tweaks.

### Why MiroFish should not pick Ayush's posts

MiroFish runs on OASIS, CAMEL-AI's simulator for up to a million LLM agents on X- and Reddit-like platforms. OASIS reproduces qualitative phenomena such as information spreading, group polarization and herd effects. But **its agents herd more than humans do, and it has no published quantitative validation against real per-post engagement** ([arXiv 2411.11581](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.11581v3)). One review notes that agents herd on dislikes but not on likes, the opposite of human behavior and possibly an artifact of the base model ([Pith review](https://pith.science/paper/2411.11581)). Critics argue that LLM agents collapse toward an "average persona" with lower behavioral variance than humans, so patterns in simulations may be model artifacts rather than social dynamics ([arXiv 2506.19806](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.19806); [arXiv 2507.19364](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.19364)). That matters because virality is a fat-tail phenomenon, and a simulator that compresses variance is structurally bad at the thing Ayush wants it for.

Direct benchmarks confirm the gap. Across more than 120,000 persona-post pairs from 1,511 real people, persona-prompted LLM agents predicted reactions at **MCC 0.29, "modest but genuine", and did not beat ordinary text classifiers** ([arXiv 2604.19787](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.19787); [Scientific Reports](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-026-66277-8)). On Upworthy headline tests, a plain LLM ranker was a medium-strength predictor on the 399 tests that had a clear winner, and **adding synthetic personas erased that advantage** ([arXiv 2609.25010](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.25010)). Zero-shot LLMs did worse than BERT at predicting retweets on X ([arXiv 2502.12073](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.12073v1)). On real Weibo and Zhihu diffusion data, hybrid LLM-plus-diffusion models beat fully agent-based simulation ([arXiv 2510.16366](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.16366v1)). OASIS also models X and Reddit recommenders, not LinkedIn's 2026 sequence ranker or its professional norms, and none of these studies covers Indian Hinglish content.

The verdict is that **MiroFish's virality scores should not be used to choose what to publish**. Two cheap uses are defensible. One is a qualitative stress test that asks what objections, misreadings or "this is fake" comments a draft might attract, which matters for story posts. The other is asking a plain LLM to rank five hook variants as a weak tie-breaker. A model trained on Ayush's own logged posts will become a better predictor within a few months than any simulator, because past author performance is the strongest pre-posting signal in the literature.

## Indian engineers share salary fights, layoff fear and family pressure

The largest creators serving Indian engineering students built trust on YouTube first and use LinkedIn and X for credibility and commentary. Apna College has about 6.3M YouTube subscribers ([vidIQ](https://vidiq.com/youtube-stats/channel/UCBwmMxybNva6P_5VmxjzwqA)), takeUforward about 1.1M ([SponsorRadar](https://sponsorradar.com/channels/takeuforward)), and Varun Mayya about 1.27M on Instagram ([CreatorDB](https://creatordb.app/creatorstats/thevarunmayya/)). No verified LinkedIn-only follower counts were found for any of them. Their identity rests on credentials and an origin story, such as the widely shared "IIT Roorkee to FAANG to quitting to teach" narrative about Harkirat Singh ([X thread](https://x.com/shobhittt007/status/2050586641614749784)).

The repeat viral themes for this audience are well documented in Indian business media, which republishes LinkedIn posts as news. **Fresher salary fights** are one: a techie's January 2025 post arguing "Rs 3.6 LPA isn't bad" for unskilled tier-3 graduates set off a debate in which commenters called it exploitation ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/latest/trends/story/rs-36-lpa-isnt-bad-if-youre-from-techies-post-on-fresher-salaries-in-it-triggers-debate-460553-2025-01-13)), against a backdrop of an average 2025 starting salary of about ₹3.5 LPA ([People Matters](https://www.peoplematters.in/news/recruiting-and-onboarding/hiring-in-india-remains-highbut-mostly-for-freshers-willing-to-settle-for-less-indeed-45358)). **Layoffs and AI anxiety** are another: TCS's roughly 12,000 job cuts in July 2025 produced LinkedIn reactions like "we thought TCS was like a government job" that became stories in their own right ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/amp/personal-finance/retirement-planning/story/we-thought-tcs-was-like-a-government-job-wealth-advisor-says-no-ones-safe-past-45-486543-2025-07-28)), and "will AI replace me" fears among recent graduates persisted into 2026 ([Newslaundry](https://www.newslaundry.com/2026/04/27/will-ai-replace-me-anxiety-grips-tech-workers-amid-mass-layoffs-slowing-recruitment)). Others are **work-hours culture**, which returns with every new Narayana Murthy remark ([Inc42](https://inc42.com/features/why-has-narayana-murthy-70-hour-work-week-pitch-stirred-controversy/amp/)), **toxic-workplace exposés** ([Credyfi](https://www.credyfi.com/news/techie-reveals-shocking-toxic-work-culture-at-bengaluru-firm)), and **Bengaluru career and #PeakBengaluru humor** ([Business Today](https://www.businesstoday.in/india/story/my-career-took-off-in-bengaluru-techies-viral-post-has-a-message-for-every-job-seeker-541972-2026-07-09); [Deccan Herald](https://www.deccanherald.com/amp/story/india%2Fkarnataka%2Fbengaluru%2Fpeak-bengaluru-moments-that-went-viral-3233809)). The common structure is a **debatable number (LPA, hours, headcount) attached to a named company, city or relationship**. That gives readers a side to take in the comments.

No study measures whether Hinglish beats English on LinkedIn. Hinglish usage on Twitter grew about 2% a year from 2014 to 2022 ([Sengupta et al., via DOAJ](https://doaj.org/article/dd04d2643250465bb49e5749ec007773)), LinkedIn launched in Hindi in 2021 with creators like Warikoo ([LinkedIn News](https://news.linkedin.com/2021/december/linkedin-launches-in-hindi)), and the top coding YouTubers teach in Hinglish. Yet the LinkedIn posts that Indian media amplify are mostly English text posts. Ayush's own Hinglish post supports a nuanced reading: it reached 11,454 people and still drew 286 impressions a week a month later, but it gained **essentially no followers above baseline**. Hinglish works as a reach device, not a follow device, unless the post also gives a professional reason to follow.

Credibility is the main risk in this niche. Blind threads describe Indian LinkedIn influencers posting staged "helping someone" stories that commenters call fake but still engage with heavily ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/linkedin-influencers-jpp8hpx2)), and r/LinkedInLunatics exists to mock exactly that genre ([Crain's](https://prod.crainsdetroit.com/node/909611)). Ayush's best format, the "my friend" story, is also the format most exposed to that callout. It works only if the stories are real, consented and specific enough to be believed, with names and companies anonymized where needed. Course-led monetization is also under pressure: Ankur Warikoo closed his ₹100 Cr+ course business in May 2026 and pointed to AI's impact ([India TV](https://www.indiatvnews.com/trending/news/ankur-warikoo-shuts-down-profitable-rs-100-crore-courses-business-hints-at-huge-ai-impact-2026-05-16-1041375)), which argues for building audience trust before choosing a product.

## X, Hacker News and YC all reward specificity and author replies

X's open-sourced ranking code turns hazy LinkedIn lore into numbers. In the 2023 Twitter release, the heavy ranker weighted a predicted like at 0.5, a reply at 13.5, a good profile click at 12.0, a **reply that the author engaged with at 75.0**, negative feedback at −74 and a report at −369 ([twitter/the-algorithm-ml](https://github.com/twitter/the-algorithm-ml/blob/main/projects/home/recap/README.md)). These weights multiply predicted probabilities, not counts. The 2026 xAI release keeps the weighted sum of predicted actions but models dwell, DM and copy-link shares, follows and a "not dwelled" negative signal directly. It also adds author-diversity decay (each additional post from the same author is discounted), an out-of-network discount and a **boost for new, low-impression authors** ([xai-org/x-algorithm](https://github.com/xai-org/x-algorithm)). LinkedIn has not published weights, but its stated priorities (meaningful comments, relevance, no bait) map closely onto the same signals. The transferable lessons are to reply to comments quickly and substantively, write posts people send privately to friends (Ayush's relatable career posts are WhatsApp-forward material), and avoid bait that triggers hides.

On links, X's head of product denied that links are deboosted and attributed their lower reach to lower engagement, while Musk said a bare link gets weak distribution and a link with an interesting description does fine ([Nieman Lab](https://www.niemanlab.org/2025/10/x-makes-overtures-to-journalists-with-new-feature-designed-to-improve-reach-for-links/)). The same applies on LinkedIn: keep the value in the post body and put any link in a comment.

Hacker News adds discipline about titles. Its guidelines ban editorializing, exclamation points and "gratuitous number or number + adjective", and require the original title ([HN Guidelines](https://news.ycombinator.com/newsguidelines.html)). Its ranking decays with age faster than it grows with votes, and a 2013 analysis found about 20% of front-page stories penalized, including a controversy penalty for threads with more comments than upvotes ([righto.com](https://www.righto.com/2013/11/how-hacker-news-ranking-really-works.html)). In a study of 138 AI repository launches on HN, posting time was the strongest factor and the "Show HN" label added nothing significant ([arXiv 2511.04453](https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.04453v1)). The lesson for LinkedIn is that **literal and specific beats hype**: "my friend went from ₹9 LPA to ₹38 LPA in 3 switches" works because it states something checkable, and "AI engineer is the most oversold title" does not.

The YC and founder canon agrees. Paul Graham's "How to Write Usefully" says usefulness is importance × novelty × correctness × strength, and claims should be as strong as possible without becoming false ([paulgraham.com](https://www.paulgraham.com/useful.html)). swyx's "Learn in Public" argues for making the resource you wished you had and shipping before it is perfect ([swyx gist](https://gist.github.com/swyxio/9720bd4a30606ca3ffb8d407113c0fe5)). Justin Welsh treats LinkedIn as primary and recycles top posts by rewriting their hooks and restructuring their bodies ([The Way Up](https://thewayup.beehiiv.com/p/how-justin-welsh-built-1-5m-followers-with-a-content-system)). Building in public has strong anecdotes, such as Pieter Levels publishing his revenue openly, but no controlled evidence and heavy survivorship bias ([Indie Hackers](https://www.indiehackers.com/post/from-google-sheets-to-3-6m-as-a-solo-founder-de782f722a)). Twitter's own 2014 study of 2 million tweets found photos raised retweets by 35% and numbers by 17% ([Associations Now](https://associationsnow.com/2014/03/study-youre-tweeting-add-photo-higher-engagement/)). Those figures are dated, but they are directionally consistent with the "concrete number plus visual" pattern.

**The lane effect is large and statistically real despite small samples.** Three of four narrative or humor posts exceeded 12x the 898-impression median. None of 20 opinion or insight posts exceeded 1.7x. A Fisher exact test on that split gives p ≈ 0.001, with the caveat that the classification was done after the fact. The one narrative post that flopped (a founder "last Tuesday at 9 pm" story, 763 impressions) was a B2B topic, which suggests the audience match matters as much as the format. His breakout rate of **12.5% of posts above 10x median is about six times the ~2% "viral" rate in industry data** ([ViralBrain Q3 2026](https://www.viralbrain.ai/blog/linkedin-intelligence-report-q3-2026)), and his top 20% of posts produced 90% of impressions, in line with the 90–93% creator norm ([Level Up](https://levelupnewsletter.beehiiv.com/p/consistency-is-the-wrong-goal-eec2)). That hit rate points to skill in a specific lane, not luck alone.

**The breakouts reached a different audience than his followers.** Over 12 months, viewers were far more senior and more often in big tech than followers: senior seniority 31% vs 11%, Software Engineer title 29% vs 9%, 10,001+ employee companies 26% vs 8%, with Microsoft, Amazon, Oracle and Google leading the viewer company list. His followers' top companies, by contrast, are Scaler School of Technology, Scaler AI Labs, GirlScript, Mirai School and BITS Pilani. Bengaluru accounts for about a quarter of both groups, and Delhi is overrepresented among followers (17% vs 10%). In the most recent quiet week, viewer demographics collapsed back toward the follower profile. The dual audience is an asset. **Students follow him, and working SWEs make his posts travel.** The best topics are the ones both groups feel: placements, salary jumps, hiring absurdity, family pressure and AI job anxiety.

**The August pivot cost him momentum on three fronts at once.** From 19 June to 14 July he posted 16 AI-career posts (about 4.3 a week) and got both big hits. Then, the day after his largest post brought around 600 new followers in four days, he went silent for **38 days**, came back at about one post a week, and tilted toward B2B/GTM content aimed at owners and CXOs, who make up about 5% of his audience. The median for non-viral posts fell 32% (from 1,028 to 696 impressions). The data cannot separate the effects of the topic change, the lower cadence and the platform-wide decline, but the only Phase 2 breakout was an off-strategy Hinglish post.

**Engagement, not distribution, is the weak spot on normal posts.** His median per-post engagement rate of 1.15% is about half the 2.4–2.7% benchmark for accounts his size ([ConnectSafely citing AuthoredUp](https://connectsafely.ai/articles/linkedin-engagement-rate-calculator-guide-2026)). Non-viral impressions per follower (~13–17% recently) are at or above the 8–16% benchmarks ([Supergrow](https://supergrow.ai/blog/what-are-linkedin-impressions); [Socialinsider](https://socialinsider.io/social-media-statistics/linkedin-statistics)). Abstract takes get shown and then scrolled past. The breakouts' low engagement rates (0.38–0.63%) are expected, because cold audiences engage less, so engagement rate is the wrong scorecard for breakouts.

**Most follower growth does not come from posts.** He gained 1,712 followers from February to May with zero posts and zero impressions, and recurring spikes of 70–155 followers a day arrive on low-impression days, clustering on Saturdays since August. Excluding viral days, the correlation between daily impressions and new followers is only r = 0.16. Breakout stories and satire converted cold viewers at about 4–6 excess followers per 1,000 impressions. The Hinglish post converted roughly zero. Normal posts produce no detectable follower lift. Gross new followers (5,911) exceed his current count (5,548), which implies at least 363 unfollows. Something off-feed, plausibly a Scaler or student community channel, people-you-may-know suggestions, or weekly events, is his biggest growth engine, and he should find out what it is.

## The playbook: three lanes, five posts a week, one log

The recommendations below combine his own data (the strongest evidence for his account) with external research. Confidence reflects both the quality of the evidence and how well it transfers to him.

|---|---|---|
| Make 70–80% of posts real-person career/money stories, hiring or workplace satire, and Hinglish engineering-life humor | 3/4 vs 0/20 split in his data; interpersonal content wins on LinkedIn ([SAGE Open](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/21582440261428891)) | High |
| Stop B2B/GTM posts; target the shared student-plus-working-SWE career experience | Lowest-performing type; owners/CXOs ≈5% of his audience | High |
| Keep every story real and consented, anonymize identities, keep checkable specifics (₹ figures, YOE, city, company type) | Callout culture ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/linkedin-influencers-jpp8hpx2)); LinkedIn's anti-inauthenticity stance | High |
| Write in his own voice; never publish templated AI drafts or AI comments | LinkedIn limits suspected AI content to 1st-degree ([Engadget](https://www.engadget.com/2174163/linkedin-doesnt-want-your-ai-slop-anymore/)) | High |

### How to measure and log

Because pre-posting prediction is weak and his own history is the best predictor, the log is the most valuable thing he can build. He should keep one row per post in a spreadsheet and fill each block on the schedule below.

| When | Fields to record |
|---|---|
| At posting | Full text; hook line (first ~140 characters); lane; hook type (character, number, question, contrarian, parody format); language (English, Hinglish, mixed); format (text, image, carousel, poll, video); target persona (student, junior SWE, senior SWE); topic; IST hour and weekday; people tagged; whether it was shared to WhatsApp, college or community groups |
| T+1h, T+3h | Impressions, reactions, comments, reposts; number of comments he replied to |
| T+24h | Same metrics; whether any larger creator commented or reposted |
| T+7d | Impressions; reactions, comments, reposts, saves and sends where shown; followers gained from the post and profile views (from per-post analytics); viewer seniority and company-size mix if shown |
| Every Monday | Total follower count (to compute net growth and unfollows); notes on any day with 70+ new followers and what happened off-platform that day (classes, Scaler or community events, newsletter mentions, comments on big creators' posts) |

He should score posts on three metrics, not on engagement rate: **impressions relative to his rolling median** (a breakout is above 5x, currently about 4,500), **followers gained per 1,000 impressions**, and **comments per 1,000 impressions**, which measures how much conversation a post started. At day 30, the decision rules are simple. If story and satire posts again beat abstract posts on median impressions and on followers per 1,000, he should lock them in as 80% of his output. If Hinglish posts with a takeaway ending convert at least half as well as stories, he should keep them at about one a week, and drop them to occasional if not. If the morning and evening medians differ by less than 30%, timing is noise and he should post whenever he can reply quickly. After 40–50 logged posts, a simple regression or an LLM classifier fine-tuned on his own rows will beat any persona simulator. Until then, the decision should be made on medians, not on model output.

## Conclusion

The research reframes Ayush's problem. He does not need to discover what goes viral; his own data, consistent with the academic and platform evidence, has already shown him the lane, and his hit rate inside it is several times the industry norm. What cost him was leaving that lane right after it paid off, chasing a B2B audience he does not have, and going silent when attention was highest. His real growth lever is that two audiences meet in his feed: students who follow him and working SWEs at big tech who carry his posts. Content about the career experiences they share (money, hiring absurdity, family expectations, AI anxiety) serves both. The 2026 LinkedIn ranker, which matches posts to each viewer's history and suppresses generic AI text, favors exactly the specific, voice-heavy, India-coded writing he does best.

The less obvious takeaway is about tools and authenticity. Virality is a fat-tailed process that neither LLM agent simulators nor content features forecast well, so the edge comes from shipping volume in a proven lane and learning from his own logged distribution, not from a prediction engine. His winning format is also his biggest reputational risk: "my friend" stories work because readers believe them, so the one rule that matters more than any hook formula is to keep them true.
